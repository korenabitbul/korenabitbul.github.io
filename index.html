How Much Training Data is Memorized in Overparameterized Autoencoders? An Inverse Problem Perspective on Memorization Evaluation

Koren Abitbul, Yehuda Dar

Degraded input

Both methods start from the same training image with a block of missing pixels. Step through the iterations with the arrows or drag along the bar. From Fig. 1 of the paper.

Autoencoders often memorize their training images, and memorization is usually measured by asking the trained model to fill in missing regions of a training image. We cast that recovery as an inverse problem: the trained autoencoder implicitly defines a regularizer for its own training set, and an iterative method alternates between applying the autoencoder and estimating the unknown degradation.

  • Blind inpainting: recovers training images with many missing pixels in an unknown pattern.
  • Evaluated on fully connected and U-Net autoencoders, across nonlinearities and training-loss levels.
  • Significantly outperforms previous recovery methods, including in settings previously considered impractical for memorization evaluation.

Blackjack Card Counter: Real-Time Card Detection and Hi-Lo Counting

Personal project · 2026 · Python, YOLO, OpenCV

A real-time computer vision pipeline that watches a blackjack table, finds every card at any angle, reads its rank, and keeps the Hi-Lo running and true count as the cards are dealt.

  • YOLO OBB detector
  • →
  • IoU tracker
  • →
  • Rank classifier
  • →
  • Hi-Lo count
  • Oriented bounding boxes handle cards at any rotation; each crop is perspective-warped before classification.
  • Multi-frame weighted voting finalizes a card's rank only after consensus, so one bad frame can't change the count.
  • Classification runs batched and asynchronous on a background thread, keeping the main loop real-time on Apple Silicon (MPS).
  • Pluggable trackers (simple IoU, ByteTrack, BoT-SORT, SORT); the IoU tracker avoids the box inflation of Kalman filters.
